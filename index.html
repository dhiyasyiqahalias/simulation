Understanding K-Means Clustering

K-Means is an unsupervised partition-based clustering algorithm that groups N data points into K distinct, non-overlapping clusters.

1. Objective Function (WCSS)

The algorithm minimizes the Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS), also known as inertia:

J = ∑ (i=1 to K) ∑ (x ∈ C_i) || x - μ_i ||²

2. Algorithm Steps

  1. Initialization: Pick K initial cluster centroids $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_k$ (e.g. via K-Means++).
  2. Assignment Step: Assign each data point $x_j$ to the cluster with the nearest centroid:
    c(j) = argmin_i || x_j - μ_i ||
  3. Update Step: Recalculate each centroid as the center of mass (mean) of all points assigned to it:
    μ_i = (1 / |C_i|) ∑ (x ∈ C_i) x
  4. Convergence: Repeat steps 2 and 3 until centroid movements fall below tolerance $\epsilon$ or maximum iterations are reached.

3. Why K-Means++?

Standard random initialization can get trapped in poor local optima. K-Means++ selects subsequent centroids with probability proportional to the squared distance $D(x)^2$ from the nearest existing centroid, providing provable $O(\log k)$ competitive bounds.

4. Limitations to Explore

Use the presets on the left to observe how K-Means behaves when assumptions are violated:

  • Concentric Rings / Moons: K-Means assumes convex, spherical clusters and fails on complex geometric manifolds.
  • Anisotropic Blobs: Standard Euclidean distance assumes isotropic (equal variance in all directions) clusters.
  • Varied Densities: Dense clusters may swallow neighboring sparse clusters.
